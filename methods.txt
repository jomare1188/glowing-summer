================================================================================
METHODS — SNP DISCOVERY IN CALLICARPA sp. WITH CONTAMINATION SCREENING
AND DEPTH-EQUALISED DATASETS
================================================================================
Written 2026-07-28. Supersedes the previous version, retained as
methods_previous.txt. Versions below were read from the installed binaries under
the activation sequence the pipeline actually uses, not from documentation.

Pipeline scripts:
  qc/qc_pipeline_v2.sh        read QC, trimming, contamination screening
  qc/setup_kraken_db.sh       Kraken2 reference database installation
  snp_calling/snp_datasets.sh alignment through to analysis-ready VCFs
  snp_calling/monitor_datasets.sh  progress monitoring (read-only)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. RATIONALE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An initial analysis of these 18 whole genomes produced population-genetic
estimates that were difficult to interpret. The cause was traced to two linked
problems. First, the proportion of reads aligning to the reference varied from
5.9% to 76.8% across samples, despite near-uniform raw sequencing effort (52-98x
of raw data per sample). Second, the resulting usable coverage therefore ranged
from 3.9x to 73.3x, a 19-fold spread. Sequencing depth of that heterogeneity
affects both which sites are callable and how confidently genotypes are assigned
at those sites, so allele frequencies and every statistic derived from them are
confounded with per-sample coverage.

The analysis presented here addresses both problems directly: the source of the
non-aligning reads is identified and quantified, samples that cannot support
reliable genotyping are excluded on a stated criterion, and the remaining samples
are downsampled to a common depth so that coverage no longer varies across the
cohort.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. READ QUALITY CONTROL AND TRIMMING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Raw paired-end reads (2 x 150 bp, Illumina) were assessed with FastQC v0.12.1 and
summarised with MultiQC v1.33. Reads were processed with fastp v1.0.1 with
adapters auto-detected from read overlap (--detect_adapter_for_pe). The first
14 bp of each read were hard-trimmed (-f 14 -F 14); poly-G and poly-X tails were
removed; a low-complexity filter was applied (--low_complexity_filter,
--complexity_threshold 30); bases below Phred 20 were treated as unqualified and
reads with more than 30% unqualified bases were discarded
(--qualified_quality_phred 20, --unqualified_percent_limit 30); overlap-based
error correction was performed for read pairs (--correction,
--overlap_len_require 30, --overlap_diff_limit 5); and reads shorter than 50 bp
after trimming were discarded (--length_required 50). Trimmed reads were
re-assessed with FastQC and MultiQC.

Per-sample yield was tabulated from the fastp JSON reports, recording raw and
retained reads and bases, the fraction of bases retained, Q30 rate, GC content,
duplication rate, and a theoretical coverage estimate (retained bases divided by
the 506,362,408 bp reference). This table is the primary record of sequencing
effort per sample and is independent of any downstream alignment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CONTAMINATION SCREENING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Because the initial mapping failures pointed to non-target DNA rather than to
read quality, a taxonomic screen was added upstream of alignment. For each
sample, 100,000 read pairs were classified with Kraken2 v2.17.1 against the
PlusPFP index (release k2_pluspfp_16_GB_20260626), which contains bacterial,
archaeal, viral, protozoan, fungal, plant and human references. A database
including plants and fungi was required: an index restricted to bacteria and
human would report both the target genome and any plant or fungal contaminant as
unclassified and would therefore be uninformative here.

The absolute proportion of classified reads is not a meaningful contamination
metric in this system. Callicarpa americana is not represented in RefSeq at the
genome level, so approximately 91-96% of reads are unclassified even in the
cleanest samples. What distinguishes samples is the composition of the classified
fraction. Reads originating from the target are assigned to the nearest available
relatives, which are consistently genera within the Lamiales (Sesamum, Salvia,
Primulina, Olea, Henckelia), and are therefore counted as Viridiplantae. Reads
originating from contamination are assigned to bacteria.

The ratio of Viridiplantae to Bacteria among classified reads is reported per
sample as a contamination index. Across the 18 samples this index is strongly
associated with the alignment rate measured subsequently (Spearman rho = 0.942;
Pearson r = 0.964 on log10-transformed ratios, n = 18), so it predicts alignment
success from raw reads alone, before any alignment is performed.

The contaminating taxa in the worst-affected samples are soil and rhizosphere
organisms: Actinomycetes (Mycobacterium, Mycolicibacterium, Streptomyces,
Nocardioides, Pseudonocardia) together with Bradyrhizobium, Sphingomonas and
Methylobacterium, and the plant-pathogenic fungus Colletotrichum. This
composition is consistent with soil carryover or a high epiphytic and endophytic
load on field-collected tissue, rather than with a library-preparation or
index-hopping artefact.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ALIGNMENT AND DUPLICATE MARKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Trimmed reads were aligned to the Callicarpa americana reference assembly
(Hamilton et al. 2020; 506,362,408 bp) with BWA-MEM v0.7.17-r1188, using -M to
flag split hits as secondary and assigning per-sample read groups. Alignments
were streamed directly into samtools v1.19.2 sort, so an unsorted BAM is never
written. Duplicates were marked with GATK MarkDuplicates v4.6.2.0
(--VALIDATION_STRINGENCY SILENT, --COMPRESSION_LEVEL 9). Alignment statistics
were collected with samtools flagstat.

Alignment is performed once, at full depth. Both depth-equalised datasets defined
below are derived from these same duplicate-marked BAMs, so no sample is ever
aligned more than once.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COVERAGE ESTIMATION AND SAMPLE EXCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Usable coverage was measured per sample from the duplicate-marked BAM as

    usable depth = bases mapped (cigar) / 506,362,408

where "bases mapped (cigar)" is reported by samtools stats and counts aligned
bases only, so soft-clipped read ends do not inflate the estimate. Duplicate,
secondary and supplementary alignments were excluded from the count (-F 0xD04)
because none of them contributes independent evidence for genotyping. This
quantity is therefore the mean depth of independent, aligned evidence available
per site, and is the value against which the depth targets below are applied.

Two datasets were defined, with minimum depth thresholds of 9x and 20x. A sample
is included in a dataset only if its measured usable depth reaches that
dataset's threshold. This is the sole exclusion criterion: no sample is removed
on the basis of its contamination index, its taxonomic profile, or its alignment
rate. Those measurements explain why a sample fails, but the decision itself
rests on whether enough independent evidence remains to genotype the sample at
the target depth. The rule is one-directional by necessity, since downsampling
can remove reads but cannot create them.

Applying this criterion to the coverage measured in the initial analysis, three
samples (557 at 3.9x, 558 at 4.7x, 553 at 6.1x) fall below the 9x threshold and
are excluded from both datasets; these are the same three samples with the lowest
plant-to-bacteria ratios in the contamination screen. A further five samples
(564, 563, 549, 555, 560) reach 9x but not 20x and so contribute to the 9x
dataset only. On those figures the 9x dataset comprises 15 samples and the 20x
dataset 10 samples. These counts are provisional: membership is recomputed from
the coverage measured during the present run, and two samples (564 at 9.11x and
563 at 9.19x) sit close enough to the 9x threshold that a small change in
trimming yield could move them. The definitive membership table is written to
results/metrics/depth_summary.tsv before any variant calling begins.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. DEPTH EQUALISATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Excluding low-coverage samples narrows the range of coverage but does not remove
the depth gradient among those retained; at the 9x threshold the retained samples
would still span roughly 9x to 73x. Each retained sample was therefore
downsampled to its dataset's target depth. For sample i with measured usable
depth d_i and dataset target T, the retained fraction is

    f_i = min(1, T / d_i)

Downsampling was performed on the duplicate-marked BAM with samtools view
--subsample f_i --subsample-seed 42. This implementation hashes read names, so
both mates of a pair are retained or discarded together and pairing is preserved.
The fixed seed makes the operation reproducible. Samples already at or below the
target (f_i >= 0.999) are passed through unchanged rather than resampled.

After this step every sample within a dataset carries the same expected depth,
and differences in genotype confidence between samples reflect genome content
rather than sequencing effort. In validation on simulated data, four samples
spanning 9.8x to 37.5x native depth converged to mean genotype depths of 8.70x,
8.62x, 8.58x and 8.78x after equalisation at a 9x target, reducing a 3.8-fold
spread to 1.02-fold.

The cost is deliberate and substantial: the highest-coverage sample retains
roughly 12% of its aligned reads in the 9x dataset. This is accepted because
uniform depth across samples is required for the population-genetic comparisons
the datasets are built to support.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. VARIANT CALLING AND JOINT GENOTYPING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both datasets were called independently and identically. Per-sample variants
were called from the downsampled BAMs with GATK HaplotypeCaller v4.6.2.0 in GVCF
mode (-ERC GVCF, --min-base-quality-score 20), which records genotype
likelihoods at every position including monomorphic sites. Per-sample GVCFs were
merged with GATK CombineGVCFs and genotyped jointly with GATK GenotypeGVCFs to
produce a cohort-wide VCF. SNPs were extracted with GATK SelectVariants
(-select-type SNP).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. VARIANT FILTERING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Site-level hard filters were applied with GATK VariantFiltration, excluding
variants with Quality by Depth (QD) < 2.0, Fisher Strand bias (FS) > 60.0, RMS
Mapping Quality (MQ) < 40.0, Strand Odds Ratio (SOR) > 3.0, MQRankSum < -12.5,
or ReadPosRankSum < -8.0. Only variants passing all filters were retained, using
BCFtools v1.19.

Population-level filters were applied with VCFtools v0.1.16, removing indels and
retaining sites in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium at p >= 0.01, with minor allele
frequency >= 0.01 and no more than 20% missing genotypes (--max-missing 0.8).

Mean site depth bounds were set relative to each dataset's target depth rather
than as fixed constants, at half and three times the target:

    9x dataset:   --min-meanDP  4   --max-meanDP  27
    20x dataset:  --min-meanDP 10   --max-meanDP  60

This scaling is required rather than cosmetic. The fixed lower bound of 20x used
in the previous analysis would, applied to the 9x dataset, remove every site,
because mean depth in that dataset is 9x by construction. The bounds retain sites
with depth consistent with the equalised target while still excluding collapsed
repeats and other regions of anomalously high coverage. All depth-independent
filters are identical across the two datasets, so differences between them are
attributable to depth and sample composition rather than to filtering.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. LINKAGE DISEQUILIBRIUM PRUNING AND MARKER SUBSETS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To limit pseudoreplication in ordination and clustering analyses, the filtered
SNP set of each dataset was pruned for linkage disequilibrium with PLINK
v1.90b6.21 using a 50-SNP sliding window, a 10-SNP step and an r^2 threshold of
0.2 (--indep-pairwise 50 10 0.2). From each pruned panel, random subsets of
50,000 and 5,000 SNPs were drawn with a fixed random seed, balancing genome-wide
representation against marker independence. Each dataset therefore yields a full
filtered SNP set, an LD-pruned panel, and 50K and 5K subsets of that panel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. INPUT PREPARATION FOR RUNS OF HOMOZYGOSITY ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ROH inputs were prepared separately for each dataset from the full filtered SNP
set, not from the LD-pruned panel. LD pruning removes precisely the correlated
markers that ROH detection relies on to trace contiguous homozygous tracts, and
the 5K and 50K subsets are far too sparse to resolve segment boundaries on a
506 Mb genome.

Biallelic SNPs were extracted with BCFtools (--min-alleles 2 --max-alleles 2
--type snps), then filtered with VCFtools for call rate (--max-missing 0.9) and
minor allele frequency at two thresholds, 0.05 and 0.10, run in parallel to allow
a sensitivity assessment. Hardy-Weinberg filtering was deliberately omitted at
this stage: inbred individuals systematically violate Hardy-Weinberg expectations
at heterozygous loci, so applying such a filter before ROH detection would remove
the signal the analysis is intended to measure. Filtered VCFs were converted to
PLINK binary format with PLINK v2.0.0-a.6.9 (--allow-extra-chr --max-alleles 2
--set-missing-var-ids '@:#' --new-id-max-allele-len 50).

Downstream ROH detection and F_ROH estimation follow the three-length-class
design (short >= 100 kb, medium >= 500 kb, long >= 1,500 kb) described separately.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. REPRODUCIBILITY AND COMPUTATIONAL NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All random operations use fixed seeds (downsampling seed 42; SNP subsetting seed
42), so both datasets are reproducible from the trimmed reads. Every stage is
checkpointed per sample and per dataset, and an interrupted run resumes without
repeating completed work. Intermediate files are removed as soon as they are no
longer required: sorted BAMs after duplicate marking, downsampled BAMs after
their GVCF is written, duplicate-marked BAMs once both datasets have been derived
from them, and the combined GVCF after joint genotyping. Per-sample GVCFs and all
final VCFs are retained.
